30 new badges: Easy, Medium, Hard

Today's 32 badges all start at real milestones (10 games finished, 25 sessions, 10 reviews), so nobody earns anything on day one. These 30 fill the ladder underneath: ten Easy badges you earn in your first session, each one teaching a feature; ten Medium side quests for your first weeks; and ten Hard boss fights that take months. None of them copy an existing badge, and every one reads data the app already records.

How difficulty reads

The medallion's color says what it's for (the same three accents the whole app uses). The dots say how hard: one dot Easy, two Medium, three Hard. Hard badges glow. Locked badges are dim with a progress ring, so you always see how close you are.

Day one, for a brand-new player

The fear was right: today a new player adds three games and gets nothing. With the Easy row, onboarding alone pays out, and the first few minutes after it keep paying.

Where they live and how they unlock

No new screen. They join the six rows the badge page already has, next to the badges they lead into. Small wins get a small moment: Easy badges show the notice capsule you approved, not the full-screen celebration. Medium and Hard badges keep the full celebration. Onboarding never fires popups one after another: everything earned there arrives together on one card at the end.

Claude's recommendation: Player Level next, built on XP. Every badge, session, finish, review and weekly quest earns XP. Your level shows on your profile and in the Pulse. Levels unlock things people can see: avatar ring colors, a title under your name, profile frames, app icons. Nothing is bought; everything is earned, so a level means something. Weekly quests come with it, because they're what make XP a daily habit. Streak freezes become a level reward, so a missed day stops being a reason to quit.

Badges are what you achieved. The level is how far you've come. The quests are why you open the app today.

Where XP comes from

XP, first pass

  • Caps on easy-to-farm actions (hearts, comments): XP for the first 10 a day, then nothing. The level can't be spammed.
  • Nothing is ever taken away. A break pauses quests; it never lowers your level.
  • Earned, not bought. Plus can add extra cosmetic styles later, but XP and levels are never for sale.

Build order, if you approve

1 · The 30 badges

  • Definitions added to the badge catalogue with an Easy, Medium or Hard level (the dots).
  • Computation in the existing badge engine, from the tables listed on each card. Kindred Spirit reuses the taste-match engine.
  • Backfill for existing players first, with one summary instead of a pile of popups (the same lesson as Sprint 16).
  • Unlock moments: capsule for Easy, full celebration for Medium and Hard, one card at the end of onboarding.

2 · Player Level, then quests

  • XP ledger table, written by the same events the badges read.
  • Level ring on Profile and a small level number beside names in the Pulse.
  • Unlock track: ring colors, titles, frames, app icons.
  • Weekly quests and streak freezes as level rewards.
  • Seasonal badges (October: finish a horror game) once the base is in.
